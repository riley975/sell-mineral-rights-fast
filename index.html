What we buy

Plain-language versions of the interest types oil and gas buyers actually underwrite.

Producing (PDP)

Wells already on production. If a royalty check shows up in your mailbox, this is usually the interest behind it. We price it from the income, the decline, and the lease — not from a generic price per acre.

Near-term: permits and DUCs

A permit is on file, or the well has been drilled but not completed (a DUC). Value is in what is about to happen, even if you are not being paid yet.

Undeveloped locations (PUD)

Proved undeveloped drilling locations. The minerals are not producing, but they sit where an operator can still develop them. Location matters more here than a check stub.

Minerals, royalties, and overrides

We buy mineral interests, royalty interests, and overriding royalty interests (ORRI). An override is carved out of a lease and generally lasts only as long as that lease does. Tell us which one you have if you know.

Inherited, small, and fractional

A family share, a few net acres, or a decimal that looks tiny on a stub is still worth a look. You do not have to round up every heir before we can offer on your piece.

Common questions

All frequently asked questions

Are there any fees to get an offer?

No. There is no cost to tell us about your interest or to receive a written cash offer, and you are never obligated to accept it. We are the buyer, so there is no broker commission.

Do you buy small, fractional, or inherited interests?

Yes. Inherited minerals, family shares, and small fractional interests are a normal part of what we buy. You do not need a large acreage position to ask for an offer.

Do I have to sell all of my mineral rights?

No. Many owners sell only part of a mineral or royalty interest and keep the rest. We can talk through what a partial sale would look like for your interest.

Where do you buy?

We buy in the Permian Basin in Texas and New Mexico (Midland and Delaware), the Red Fork and Cherokee plays in the Oklahoma Anadarko Basin, the DJ Basin in Weld County, Colorado, and the Powder River Basin in Converse County, Wyoming. We occasionally buy Bakken interests in Montana and North Dakota. Other areas are outside our current buy box.

How long does closing take?

After you accept a written offer, most closings finish within a few weeks. Title work in the county is usually what sets the pace. We coordinate the curative work and the deed, and you choose whether to move ahead.
