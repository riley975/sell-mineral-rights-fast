Selling

How do you decide what an interest is worth?

We look at royalty income, decline, nearby permits and drilling, the lease, and your decimal or net acres. The result is a written offer for your interest, not a price per acre. More is on what mineral rights are worth.

Is there a fee to request an offer?

No. There is no charge to send your information or to receive an offer, and there is no commission. We are the buyer.

Do I need a broker?

No. You can deal with us directly. Hiring a broker is your choice. We do not charge a broker fee.

Do I have to sell all of my mineral rights?

No. Many owners sell part of an interest and keep the rest. The offer states what would transfer.

What if I decide not to sell?

Then you do not sell. An offer is not a contract until you accept it. Declining does not cost you anything.

Your interest

Do you buy small or fractional interests?

Yes. Small fractions and inherited shares are a regular part of what we buy.

Do you buy if I already receive royalty checks?

Yes. Producing interests are a core part of what we buy, along with near-term development and undeveloped locations.

Do you buy overriding royalty interests?

Yes. Tell us if what you own is an override rather than a mineral interest. Overrides come out of a lease and generally last only as long as that lease.

Do you buy inherited mineral rights?

Yes, including interests split among heirs. See selling inherited mineral rights. We are not estate attorneys.

What if I do not know exactly what I own?

Start with the state, the county, a well name, or any letter from an operator. We can research public records from there.

Where we buy

Where do you buy?

We buy oil and gas mineral interests, royalties, and overriding royalties in producing areas across the United States. A large share of our acquisitions are in the Permian Basin, the Red Fork and Cherokee of the Anadarko Basin, the DJ Basin in Weld County, the Powder River Basin in Converse County, and the Bakken.

Do you buy in Louisiana, the Eagle Ford, the Barnett, and other plays?

Yes. Those areas are part of what we purchase, along with the Haynesville and other U.S. fields. Choose your state on the form, or call (817) 818-1034.

Are you limited to the counties named on the state pages?

No. The counties named on those pages are examples, not a boundary. Give us the county where the minerals are located.

Closing

How long does the process take?

A written offer usually goes out within a few business days once we can evaluate the interest. After you accept, most closings finish within a few weeks. The full sequence is on how it works.

What happens after I accept?

We order title, prepare the deed, and stay in touch until you are paid. You can have your own attorney review the documents. We are not your lawyer.

Which documents help?

Recent check stubs, a division order, a lease, a deed, or probate papers. None of them are required to request an offer.

Will I owe tax on the sale?

We cannot tell you. A mineral sale can have tax consequences that depend on your basis and how you acquired the interest. Talk with your own tax advisor before you accept an offer.
