Selling

How do you decide what an interest is worth?

We look at current royalty income, how production is declining, nearby permits and drilling, lease terms, your decimal or net acres, and what similar interests in that area are trading for. The result is a written offer for your interest, not a generic per-acre quote. More on this is on what mineral rights are worth.

Are there any fees to get an offer?

No. There is never a charge to submit your information or to receive a cash offer, and you do not owe a commission. We are the buyer.

Do I need a broker?

No. You can deal with us directly. Hiring a broker is your choice, not a requirement, and we do not charge a broker’s fee either way.

Do I have to sell all of my mineral rights?

No. Many owners sell part of a mineral or royalty interest and keep the rest. The offer spells out what would transfer.

What if I decide not to sell?

Then you don’t. An offer is not a contract until you accept it, and declining does not cost you anything.

Your interest

Do you buy small or fractional interests?

Yes. Small fractions and inherited shares are a regular part of what we buy, including interests that are awkward to sell through a listing process.

Do you buy if I am already receiving royalty checks?

Yes. Producing interests — PDP, proved developed producing — are a core part of the buy box, along with near-term development and undeveloped locations.

Do you buy overriding royalty interests?

Yes. Tell us if what you own is an override (ORRI) rather than a mineral interest. Overrides are carved out of a lease and generally last only as long as that lease.

Do you buy inherited mineral rights?

Yes, including interests split among heirs. See selling inherited mineral rights for how probate and multiple owners usually work. We are not estate attorneys.

What if I don’t know exactly what I own?

Start with the county, a well name, or any letter from an operator. We can research public records from there. Paperwork is helpful and not required to start.

Where we buy

Which states and basins do you buy in?

The Permian Basin in Texas and New Mexico, both the Midland Basin and the Delaware Basin. Red Fork and Cherokee in the Anadarko Basin of Oklahoma. The DJ Basin in Weld County, Colorado. The Powder River Basin in Converse County, Wyoming. Occasionally the Bakken in Montana and North Dakota.

Do you buy in Louisiana, the Haynesville, the Barnett, or the Eagle Ford?

No. Those areas are outside our current buy box, including Haynesville and South Louisiana. The old Louisiana page is still online so the address does not disappear, and it explains that directly. If you own minerals there and want a yes-or-no, call (817) 818-1034.

Do you buy SCOOP, STACK, or Arkoma interests in Oklahoma?

Our Oklahoma focus is the Red Fork and Cherokee plays in the Anadarko Basin. If your interest is in another Oklahoma play, call and we will tell you whether it fits. We would rather say no quickly than imply that every Oklahoma basin is in the buy box.

What if my state is not on the list?

Choose “Other” on the form or call. We will tell you if we can evaluate it. Most interests outside the basins above are not a fit.

Closing

How long does the process take?

A written offer usually goes out within a few business days once we have enough to evaluate the interest. After you accept, most closings are completed within a few weeks. County title work is the part that varies. The full sequence is on how it works.

What happens after I accept?

We order title, clear what can be cleared, prepare the deed, and stay in touch until you are paid. You can have your own attorney review the documents. We are not your lawyer.

Which documents help?

Recent royalty check stubs, a division order, an oil and gas lease, a deed, or probate papers. Bring whatever you have. None of it is required to ask for an offer.

Will I owe tax on the sale?

We can’t tell you. A mineral sale can have income-tax consequences that depend on your basis and how you acquired the interest. Talk with your own tax advisor before you accept an offer.
