Louisiana is different: mineral servitudes

Louisiana follows its own Mineral Code. When minerals are separated from the land, the right is generally a mineral servitude, and the Mineral Code provides that a servitude is extinguished by prescription of nonuse for ten years. That makes production history and well activity especially important when we evaluate Louisiana interests.

Louisiana uses parishes instead of counties. Records are kept with the clerk of court in each parish, and well and production data is available through the state’s SONRIS database.

Whether a servitude remains in effect depends on specific facts. Consult a Louisiana attorney for advice about your interest.
