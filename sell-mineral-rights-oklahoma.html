Red Fork and Cherokee, in the Anadarko

The Red Fork and Cherokee are Pennsylvanian sands across the Anadarko Basin of western Oklahoma. They have been drilled for a long time, and a lot of the ownership is inherited, fractional, and scattered across more than one section. That is the Oklahoma interest we buy: producing royalties, near-term wells, and undeveloped locations in those plays, plus overriding royalties where the lease supports them.

SCOOP, STACK, and the Arkoma Basin are not the buy box. If your minerals are there, call (817) 818-1034 and we will tell you whether this is a fit. We will not dress up a statewide “we buy Oklahoma” claim.

Section, township, and range

Oklahoma descriptions usually run by section, township, and range. Your decimal may be on a division order or a check stub from the operator. If you have a pooling notice, it often names the section. Any of those is a useful place to start, and none of them is required before you call.

Pooling notices

Oil and gas spacing and pooling in Oklahoma run through the Oklahoma Corporation Commission. When an operator pools a unit, unleased owners get a limited time to choose among the options in the order. Selling is a separate decision from that election. Deadlines in a pooling order are real, and the consequences of an election are legal questions. Read the order and talk with an Oklahoma oil and gas attorney if you are unsure. We can still tell you what we would pay for the interest so you know that number while you decide.

Deeds

Mineral deeds are recorded with the county clerk in the county where the minerals are. We review that record, and the production data, before a written offer. The offer is free to request and free to decline. See how closing works.

Inherited, small, and fractional interests are welcome. No fees and no obligation to sell.

Questions about Oklahoma

Do you buy SCOOP, STACK, or Arkoma minerals?

Those are not our focus. We buy Red Fork and Cherokee in the Anadarko Basin. If your interest is in another Oklahoma play, call and we will tell you whether we can look at it.

I received a pooling notice. Can I still ask for an offer?

Yes, you can ask. Pooling elections have deadlines and legal consequences set out in the order. Read the order and consider talking with an Oklahoma oil and gas attorney before you elect. An offer from us is one option to weigh. It is not legal advice about the election.

Do you buy small Oklahoma mineral interests?

Yes. Oklahoma minerals are often split across many owners and many sections. A small net-acre position is still something we will evaluate.
