Selling Inherited Mineral Rights

Inherited a mineral or royalty interest? Here’s how selling works, even with multiple heirs or incomplete paperwork.

Get My Free Cash Offer

Inherited minerals are common

Many mineral owners never bought their minerals. They inherited them from a parent or grandparent, often as a small share split among several heirs. If you’ve started receiving royalty checks, a division order, or letters from an operator or buyer, you may own an interest.

Can I sell before probate is finished?

It depends on your situation. Title must show that you own the interest before it can transfer. Depending on the state and the estate, that might require probate, an affidavit of heirship, or other documents. We review what’s needed and coordinate the title work as part of closing.

What if there are multiple heirs?

Each heir can usually sell their own share independently. You don’t need every family member to agree for us to make you an offer on your portion. If the family wants to sell together, we can make offers to each owner.

What if I don’t know what I inherited?

That’s normal. Start with what you have: the county, an old check stub, a division order, a will, or a deed. We can research public records to identify the interest.

Why owners sell inherited minerals

  • Small, irregular checks that aren’t worth the paperwork or tax filings.
  • Simplifying an estate or splitting value evenly among heirs.
  • Turning an interest in a far-away county into cash now.

Keep or sell?

Some owners keep inherited minerals as long-term income. Others prefer cash now. Either is reasonable. Getting an offer tells you what your option is worth. There’s no obligation.

We are not attorneys. For questions about probate or estate law, talk with an attorney licensed in the state where the minerals are located.
