There is no single price per acre

Anyone quoting one number for “mineral rights in Texas” or “a Weld County acre” has not looked at your interest. Value follows the wells, the lease, and your share of them. Be wary of a per-acre figure that arrives before those details do.

What we look at

  • Current royalty income. For a producing interest, recent checks are the starting point. The stub shows your decimal, volumes, price, and deductions.
  • Decline. How long the wells have produced, and how fast the volumes are falling, changes how much future income is left.
  • Your share. Net mineral acres or the decimal interest on a division order determines how much of a well you actually own.
  • The lease. Royalty rate, whether costs are deducted, and whether the minerals are leased, held by production, or open.
  • What is not drilled yet. A permit, a drilled-but-uncompleted well, or an undeveloped location can be worth more than today’s check — or nothing, if the rock and the operator’s plans do not support it.
  • Commodity prices and the local market. Oil and gas prices move what buyers will pay. So does how actively that county is being drilled right now.

Producing, near-term, and undeveloped

Producing interests (PDP) are usually valued from the income they generate and how that income is expected to decline. Near-term interests — a permit, or a well that has been drilled but not completed — are valued on what that well is expected to become. Undeveloped locations (PUD) are valued on the chance and the timing of future wells, which is why the same “open” minerals in two townships can be priced nothing alike.

Overrides (ORRI) are priced off the lease they come out of. They generally end when that lease ends, so the remaining life of the lease is part of the value.

What to pull off a check stub

A useful stub shows the well name, your decimal interest, production month, volumes, price, taxes, and any deductions. Three recent months tell a clearer story than one. If you do not have stubs, the county and the owner name on the last division order or deed are enough for us to start looking.

Common questions

Can you quote a price per acre?

Not honestly, not without looking at the interest. Two owners in the same county can be far apart because of production, lease terms, and what is left to drill.

Does a royalty check tell you the value?

It is the starting point for a producing interest, not the whole answer. Decline, deductions, remaining locations, and the lease all change what a buyer will pay for that income.

Is an offer an appraisal?

No. A cash offer is what we are willing to pay to buy the interest. It is not an appraisal, a fairness opinion, or tax advice.
