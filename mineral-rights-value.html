There is no single price per acre

A number for a whole state, or for an acre in a county, is not a review of your interest. Value follows the wells, the lease, and your share of them.

What we look at

  • Current royalty income. For a producing interest, recent checks are the starting point. The stub shows your decimal, volumes, price, and deductions.
  • Decline. How long the wells have produced, and how fast volumes are falling, changes how much income is left.
  • Your share. Net mineral acres, or the decimal on a division order, shows how much of a well you own.
  • The lease. Royalty rate, deductions, and whether the minerals are leased, held by production, or open.
  • What is not drilled yet. A permit, a drilled but uncompleted well, or an undeveloped location can matter as much as today’s check.
  • Prices and local activity. Oil and gas prices, and how actively the county is being drilled, change what buyers will pay.

Producing, near-term, and undeveloped

Producing interests are usually valued from current income and how that income is expected to decline. Near-term interests, such as a permit or a well that has been drilled but not completed, are valued on what that well is expected to become. Undeveloped locations are valued on the chance and timing of future wells.

Overrides are priced from the lease they come out of. They generally end when that lease ends.

What a check stub shows

A useful stub shows the well name, your decimal, the production month, volumes, price, taxes, and deductions. A few recent months are more useful than one. If you do not have stubs, the county and the owner name are enough for us to start.

Common questions

Can you quote a price per acre?

Not without looking at the interest. Two owners in the same county can be far apart because of production, lease terms, and what is left to drill.

Does a royalty check show the value?

It is the starting point for a producing interest. Decline, deductions, remaining locations, and the lease also change what a buyer will pay.

Is an offer an appraisal?

No. A cash offer is the price we will pay to buy the interest. It is not an appraisal, a fairness opinion, or tax advice.
