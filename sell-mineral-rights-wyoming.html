Converse County, Powder River Basin

The Powder River Basin in northeastern Wyoming is an active oil basin, and Converse County is where our Wyoming buying is aimed. We buy mineral interests, royalty interests, and overriding royalties there: PDP, permits and DUCs, and undeveloped locations.

Campbell County and other Powder River counties are not automatically in or out. Call with the county. If the interest is in a different Wyoming basin, it is outside what we are buying now, and we will tell you that.

How these interests are usually owned

Powder River minerals are often fractional, sometimes inherited, and sometimes described alongside a surface estate the family sold years ago. The mineral owner and the surface owner do not have to be the same person. We buy the mineral or royalty side. A check stub, a division order, or a deed that names Converse County is a strong start.

Records

The Wyoming Oil and Gas Conservation Commission holds well and production records. Deeds are recorded with the county clerk in the county where the minerals are — for this buy box, the Converse County clerk. We review both before we send a written offer.

What happens next

You get a written number, usually within a few business days of an evaluation. You can sell all of your interest or ask about selling part. There is no fee and no obligation. The closing steps are the same here as in Texas: we coordinate title, you decide whether to sign, and you should have your own attorney if you want the deed reviewed.

Questions about Wyoming

Do you buy across the Powder River Basin or only Converse County?

Converse County is the focus. If your interest is in another Powder River county, call and we will tell you if we can evaluate it. The rest of Wyoming is outside the current buy box.

Do you buy federal or fee minerals?

We buy private mineral and royalty interests, including interests under fee land. If you are not sure whether yours is fee, federal, or a leasehold override, tell us what the document says and we will sort out whether it is something we can buy.

Are fractional Wyoming interests too small?

No. Inherited and fractional shares are welcome.
