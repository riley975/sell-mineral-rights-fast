There’s no single price per acre

Two owners in the same county can get very different offers. Value depends on what is producing, what could be drilled, and the details of your ownership. Be cautious of anyone quoting a flat per-acre number without looking at your interest.

What drives value

  • Current royalty income. If your interest is producing, recent monthly royalty income is the starting point for its value.
  • Production history and decline. How long wells have produced and how fast they’re declining affects how much future income is left.
  • Your ownership share. Your net mineral acres or decimal interest (on your check stub or division order) determines your share of production.
  • Lease terms. Royalty rate, deductions, and whether the minerals are leased, open, or held by production all matter.
  • Location and geology. The county, the formation, and the operator’s activity there drive what future wells might produce.
  • Nearby drilling activity. Permits, rigs, and new wells near your tract can add value even if you aren’t receiving checks yet.
  • Market conditions. Oil and gas prices and recent market activity in your area influence what buyers will pay.

Producing vs. non-producing minerals

Producing interests are usually valued on the income they generate today and how long that income should last. Non-producing interests are valued on their development potential, which depends heavily on location and nearby activity.

How to find your interest on a check stub

Your royalty statement usually shows the well name, your decimal interest, production volumes, price, and deductions. Having a few recent stubs lets us evaluate your interest faster and more accurately.
