Delaware Basin, not the whole state

The Permian does not stop at the Texas line. The Delaware Basin runs through Lea County and Eddy County, New Mexico, and that is the New Mexico interest we buy: minerals, royalties, and overriding royalties, whether they are producing, permitted, drilled but uncompleted, or still undeveloped locations.

The San Juan Basin and other New Mexico plays are not part of the current buy box. If that is where your minerals are, call and we will tell you rather than collect a lead we cannot use.

What owners in Lea and Eddy usually have

A lot of New Mexico mineral ownership is fractional and inherited, sometimes described by section, township, and range under the federal survey. A division order decimal and a section number are often enough to identify the tract. Small shares are welcome. You do not need to own the whole section, and you do not need every cousin to sell before we can offer on your piece.

Where the records are

The New Mexico Oil Conservation Division regulates oil and gas wells and holds production and permitting records. Deeds are recorded with the county clerk in Lea County or Eddy County, depending on where the minerals sit. We use both when we evaluate an interest.

The Texas side of the same basin

Reeves, Loving, and the other Texas Delaware counties are the other half of this basin. If you own on both sides of the line, mention both counties. Background on the whole basin is on the Permian page, and the Texas counties are on the Texas page.

Questions about New Mexico

Is the New Mexico buy box the whole state?

No. Our New Mexico focus is the Delaware Basin, especially Lea and Eddy counties. The San Juan Basin and the rest of the state are outside the current buy box.

Do you buy producing and non-producing interests?

Yes. Producing royalties, near-term wells (permits and DUCs), and undeveloped Permian locations are all in range, including small and inherited shares.

Where are New Mexico mineral deeds recorded?

With the county clerk in the county where the minerals are located. Well and production records are kept by the New Mexico Oil Conservation Division.
